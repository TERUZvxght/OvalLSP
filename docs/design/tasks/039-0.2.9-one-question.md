# Task 039: 0.2.9 — one question, asked once, answered honestly

**The branch is `feat/0.2.9`.** (Named here per CLAUDE.md's residency
rule, and on `main` in `036` and `AGENTS.md`.)

**`037`'s C2.** C3 and C9 were scoped here too and are not in it — see
"Where C3 and C9 went" below, decided by the maintainer once C2's size
was known rather than guessed at up front.

## Why this release exists

`024.102` sorted the open register by cause. Ten entries share one:

> A check asserts absence from an enumeration it could not complete; and
> the four features answer from different code paths, so they disagree at
> one position.

`024.13`, `024.18`, `024.35`, `024.78`, `024.82`, `024.83`, `024.88`,
`024.91`, `024.99`, `024.100` — and 0.2.8's drive round added six more in
a single session: `024.103` through `024.108`. That is the argument the
register alone could not make.

The sharpest is `024.103`: two classes of your own sharing a short name,
and a bare reference from inside one namespace answers with the other —
so the call that works is reported and the call that raises is not.
Section 0.4's own example, on a layout as ordinary as `Billing::Comment`
beside an ActiveRecord `Comment`.

## The preventing logic

One query per position, answering **present / absent / unknown** plus
visibility, where `unknown` is produced by whatever failed to enumerate
rather than inferred by a caller.

The inversion is the whole of it. Today a consumer assumes absence and
subtracts the ways of not knowing it has heard about: 0.2.6 added four
such subtractions to `Diagnostics::Engine`, one per review round, each
after a false report on working code. With the default the other way
round, a way of not knowing nobody has thought of yet produces silence.

## Sequence, and why it is a sequence

`024.15` (0.1.12: 47 files, four rounds, zero net progress, rolled back
whole) and `024.47` (0.2.1: a rule centralised into resolution, rolled
back) are what this shape has cost here. And 0.2.8's own `Cref`
introduced the defect it existed to prevent, because one predicate ended
up answering two questions — the same failure, one layer down.

So: one step at a time, each with a corpus measurement, and **a step that
does not move a measurement is one to abandon rather than defend.**

1. `Semantic::MemberAvailability`, and `MethodResolver#availability`
   answering it with the reasons the resolver can see. `#resolve` is
   untouched, so nothing changes yet — the measurement is that the corpus
   does *not* move. **Done.**
2. Move `Diagnostics::Engine`'s reasons for declining into the resolver,
   one at a time: each turns a `closed_nominal?` guard into an `unknown`
   the resolver produces. The corpus must not move at any step; where it
   does, that guard was not what it said. **Four of six done** — a chain
   that does not reach `BasicObject`, a link with no name, a class that
   declares `method_missing`, and a surface opened by an unreadable
   macro. The corpus came out byte-identical, which is the result this
   step wanted: they were what they said they were.

   The two left both need the signature environment, to say whether an
   ancestor is one RBS declares. They move when the resolver is given
   one.

   **And one of the six could not fire.** `closed_nominal?` opened with
   `return false if entries.empty?`, and an ancestor chain is never empty
   — it holds the receiver itself for a name nothing declares, for the
   empty string, in both modes. Verified before dropping it rather than
   carried over. CLAUDE.md: a guard no input can reach is the same defect
   as an untested one, and this one had been read as a safety net since
   0.2.0.
3. `unknown-method` reports only on `absent`. **Done.** The two
   remaining reasons needed the signature environment, and it is passed
   *per call* rather than held: holding it would have meant two
   references to one fact, since `Server` rebuilds its environment when
   the client names a root other than the cwd (`024.98`) — the class of
   defect this release is about, arriving inside the fix for it. That was
   caught while writing it, by 45 specs going red at once.

   The corpus came out byte-identical again. Six reasons moved, an
   `absent` that has to be earned, and not one answer changed — which is
   the result this step wanted, and the reason each step carries a
   measurement rather than an argument.

   Two things the move exposed, both recorded where they were found: the
   engine's opening `return false if entries.empty?` could not fire (a
   chain always holds the receiver), and treating "the entry has a kind"
   as accounted-for made a resolver without signatures answer `absent`
   for every method on every class — the synthesised `Object`/`Kernel`/
   `BasicObject` tail carries a kind while its members live in RBS.
   `Diagnostics::Engine#ancestor_known?` has the same hole and never
   meets it, because `#unknown_method_findings` returns before asking
   when there is no signature environment.
4. Completion, hover and signature help onto the same query, with
   visibility. **The visibility half is done** — `024.105`, `024.107` and
   `024.108` close, and the class-side half of `024.99` with them.

   Three separate holes in one rule, each measured by a 0.2.8 review
   round asking a *booted* application `respond_to?` for every label
   offered:

   - a `def` recorded as a singleton method was given `visibility: nil`
     outright, so `private` inside `class << self` and
     `private_class_method` had nothing to filter on. The distinction
     that was missing is *why* it is singleton: inside `class << self`
     the surrounding section applies, exactly as a class body's does;
     written `def self.x` it does not, and this engine already had that
     right.
   - protected was not excluded on an explicit receiver, though private
     was — and `Prot.new.guarded` raises exactly as a private call does.
     It is filtered on the explicit-receiver branch only, because it is
     the one visibility that depends on where the call is written.
   - an alias never reached completion at all, though `#resolve` has
     followed one since Task 009 — so hover, definition and the check
     knew `aka` while completion said it did not exist. `024.100`'s shape
     exactly.

   Corpus byte-identical again, and driven directly: an explicit receiver
   on a class with a public, a private and a protected method now offers
   the public one alone.

   **Signature help** is done too, for the half that was a wrong answer:
   a method overriding another showed its signature twice, because
   `#resolve` answers the override *and* the one it overrides -- that
   ordering is what makes go-to-definition land on the nearest -- and
   both became labels. Ruby calls exactly one. Deduplicated on the
   rendered label rather than truncated to the first, because a Union
   receiver legitimately produces two different signatures for one name
   and that is what the popup is for.

   **What is left of this step, and why it is not here.** `024.100`'s
   hover half -- `p.update` hovering as nothing while completion offers
   it and the check accepts it -- is on the Active Record API path, which
   needs a connected Runtime Agent to reproduce. Driving it properly is a
   fixture this release does not have; the entry stays open with the
   round's own measurements in it rather than a guess from me.
5. C3 and C9 — **moved to 0.2.10.** See below.

## Review

Three rounds finding defects, then ship with what is open recorded. The
lesson 0.2.7 and 0.2.8 both taught: **a check is a claim, and it gets a
round aimed at whether it can fail at all.**

### Round 11 (`attack`) — eight defects, three of them made in this release

Recorded here rather than kept in a session, which is where the previous
rounds' lists lived: a list that exists only in a conversation is lost at
the next compaction, and the round's own finding about repeated guards
was nearly lost that way. Disposition as of the fix pass:

| # | What it was | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `024.107`'s alias fix reopened `024.105`: an alias has no declaration, so the visibility lookup read `nil` and a private alias was offered. **The same place twice** | Fixed — *and mechanised*: `MethodResolver#visibility_of` is now the one place that answers what a name's visibility is, so the filter cannot be right about declarations and wrong about aliases. `AliasFact` carries a `visibility`, because `private :aka` has nothing else to write on |
| 2 | `024.105`'s fix covered 2 of 4 spellings. `apply_visibility_arguments` returned early on `declares_singleton?`, under a stated reason that stopped being true in this same release | Fixed — the early return is a `kind` selection, and `private :x` reaches alias facts as well as declarations |
| 3 | `#availability` answered `absent` for members that exist (`to_s`, `send`, `Comparable#between?`): `accounted_for?` asked only whether an ancestor is *declared*, never whether it *has the member* | Fixed — `#declared_by_signatures?` consults `method_signatures`. Fixtures now name members that distinguish the two |
| 4 | Four specs that pass under either candidate behaviour | Partly fixed: the override-signature example reused the parent's parameter names, so a dedup-on-label passed it without ever choosing the callable one. The remaining three were not written down before the round's list was lost, which is most of what `024.109` records |
| 5 | Dead `MethodResolver#initialize(signatures:)` and a 14-line comment describing the rejected design as shipped | Fixed |
| 6 | Signature dedup keyed on the rendered label, so an override that *renames* a parameter — the ordinary case — still showed the phantom choice | Fixed — one entry per receiver *member*, taking its lowest-ranked candidate, which is what "Ruby calls exactly one" actually means |
| 7 | `return false unless context.method_resolver` written twice in a row, and repeated at a second reader | Fixed — `SemanticContext` refuses a nil required collaborator, so neither reader needs the guard. Containment where the value is made, not at each caller |
| 8 | Two blanket `rescue StandardError`s around a call that already contains its own failure | Fixed — both deleted; `environment_spec` now pins the containment where it lives, and fails if it is removed |

Findings 1, 3 and 6 are defects this release introduced. That is the
round working; what would not be working is fixing 1 by hand a third
time, which is why it got a countermeasure instead.

### The hunk sweep, and what "unpinned" meant this time

`ruby scripts/hunk_sweep.rb main`, run with nothing else touching the
tree: **15 hunks over `core/lib`, 10 pinned, 3 unpinned, 2 load-bearing**
(the `require_relative` and the new `member_availability.rb` — reverse
-applying either stops the suite loading). All four spec files added by
the change set pin something.

The three unpinned hunks are all **deletions of code that could not
affect behaviour**, which is a shape the sweep reports as unpinned by
construction: restoring dead code changes nothing, so nothing fails.

| hunk | what it removes | what makes the removal safe, and where that is pinned |
|---|---|---|
| h02 | `return [] unless context.method_resolver` in `#argument_count_findings` | `SemanticContext` refuses a nil resolver — `semantic_context_spec`, and the sweep reports that hunk (h04) pinned |
| h03 | the second of two identical `return false unless context.method_resolver` lines in `#closed_nominal?` | same |
| h05 | `rescue StandardError` around `Signatures::Environment#ancestors` | the environment answers `[]` rather than raising — `environment_spec` fails if that containment is removed, checked by hand |

Recorded rather than dismissed, because "it is only a deletion" is
exactly the sentence an unpinned line would want said about it. What
distinguishes these three is that each names the spec that pins *the
thing that replaced it*, and each of those specs was watched failing
first. A removal whose replacement is unpinned would not have that
column filled in.

## Where C3 and C9 went

**0.2.10**, decided once C2's size was measurable rather than estimated
in advance.

They are one thing, which is half the reason. C3 gives a published answer
the identity of the document it was computed from instead of a version
integer; that also refuses a *slower analysis of the same buffer*, which
the current rule accepts — and whether those should be published at all
is C9's question. 0.2.8's drive round measured 22 of them for one method
name typed on a 4,006-line file, with the panel clean 6.55 s after the
developer stopped. (re-measured for 0.2.10 in `040`, in this scenario rather than a different one: **it reproduces** — 12 keystrokes, 12 publishes, all 12 reporting the unfinished name; after C9, one)

The other half is `024.57`: a first attempt at C9 was built and **rolled
back**. `029` named `M-3` as the precondition it had been missing, and
that precondition shipped in 0.2.7 — so the attempt can be made again,
but it needs its own measurement and its own review loop rather than a
seat at the end of somebody else's.

What ships here is what was measured here: an `absent` that has to be
earned, six reasons living where the enumeration happens, and completion
that stops offering what cannot be called.
