## The two withheld changes, and why each stayed out

Both were built by an agent, read by a second, and measured. Neither is a
judgement call.

**`024.38`'s single capture.** The entry asks for it and the reasoning is
sound for a walk that finishes: every earlier capture is overwritten
before anything reads it, and each copies the whole environment. The walk
is budgeted precisely because it may not finish. At the *default* budget
of 5,000 — no production caller passes a smaller one — a `db/seeds.rb`
shape with 2,500 top-level statements gives `["admin"]` before and `[]`
after. Identical through 2,400 and diverging from 2,500, at two steps per
statement. The direction is to keep one capture and make it survive the
abort.

**`024.220`'s second checker.** The agent's worktree was branched from a
commit before the session checker landed, so it wrote another one — with
no timeout, where the shipped one bounds every child. Nothing was wrong
with the work; it was answering a question already answered.

## What the release cost, in the only unit that matters

Eleven user-visible fixes, and three fixes built and *not* shipped
because measurement said they made the product worse. That ratio is the
release: `024.13`'s repair removes eleven false reports over a gem corpus
and silences four real typo reports; `024.224`'s shared identity path
silences a true mismatch as soon as one unrelated class shares a last
segment; `024.38`'s optimisation loses every local when the walk hits its
budget.

## Three things about method, each of which cost something here

**A verifier handed the same fixture is not an independent measurement.**
`024.19` and `024.35` were reported fixed by a triage agent and by the
agent checking it, and both were wrong in the same way — they drove a
spelling the entry did not name. `024.19`'s rooted and namespaced
spellings really are fixed; its title and its published limitation are
about the bare one. The cheap countermeasure, for whoever runs the next
such pass: a refutation must re-derive its reproduction from the entry's
own text and from the paragraph it documents, not from the report it is
checking.

**An entry can state its own defect backwards, and a fix that trusts the
title repairs the wrong side.** `024.242` said a class held in a local
loses an overload; the narrow answer is correct and it was the constant
spelling asserting a return the signature forbids. `024.240` named two
handlers where one was at fault. Both were this project's own writing,
both were caught by driving the product, and both are corrected in place
because closing an entry freezes its cause into the record.

**Agent worktrees were not branched from the commit the brief named.**
Twice — `bdd0ebe` where the brief said `8d39437`, and again in Wave 3.
One agent noticed and diffed against its real base; the ones that did not
produced the duplicate above, and a patch that would have reverted 127
files. Every brief since tells the agent to run `git rev-parse HEAD` and
believe that instead. Separately: a patch handed back as a string was
corrupted twice, once so badly it would not apply at its own base.
Patches written to files were not corrupted once.
