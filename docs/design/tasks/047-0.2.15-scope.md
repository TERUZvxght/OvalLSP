# 047 — 0.2.15: the accuracy line, given a boundary

**0.2.15 exists because 0.3.0 could not be two things.** `045` calls
0.3.0 "the first release that may add capability" and lists nine
promises; the register simultaneously aimed 35 open defects at it. A
release cannot be the one that adds capability and the one that absorbs
everything unscheduled, and `024.153` measured what the absence of that
decision had produced.

## The rule this release runs under, and it is new

`docs/PUBLISHING.md` gained two clauses in 0.2.14, and both are the
maintainer's:

- **A patch closes a gap against *any* earlier claim**, not only the
  immediately preceding release's. Written as "the previous one" until
  then, which made a patch nearly impossible to construct: the only way
  to find a defect in `0.N.0` is to be working on `0.N+1`, by which
  point it belongs to a release two behind. Bug fixes had nowhere to go.
- **A minor ships with no open, user-visible defect that has no
  `target:`.** Anything not fixed either names the release that will fix
  it or becomes a `NOT YET` row.

The second is what makes the first coherent. The patch definition
presupposes an earlier release claimed more than it did, and nothing
constrained how much more — so the patch stream was structurally
guaranteed and each `0.N.0` was implicitly permitted to ship
known-broken.

## What this release currently holds, and why that is not yet a plan

**57 open entries target 0.2.15.** That is not a release. It is the
result of giving every previously untargeted entry a target, and of
moving the accuracy work off 0.3.0 — both correct individually, and
together they have relocated the queue rather than dissolved it.

**This is stated rather than smoothed over**, because the failure it
replaces was exactly a queue nobody could see: 26 entries in no release
at all, 18 of them user-visible and published as limitations with no one
undertaking to fix them. A visible queue with a target on every item is
better than an invisible one, and it is not the same thing as a scoped
release.

### The triangulation is done, and here is what it found

170 raw findings from the three rounds went to 13 agents, each told to
prove *fixed*, *live* or *not-a-defect* against HEAD rather than assume.

| verdict | count |
|---|---|
| live | 110 |
| fixed during 0.2.14 | 21 |
| not a defect | 1 |

The 110 live findings group into **64 new entries** (`024.154`–`024.217`)
plus **ten instances folded into `024.151`** rather than given numbers of
their own — each of those ten is the same class in a different check,
and the register's rule is one entry per defect.

**None of the 64 is user-visible.** They are defects in what this
project uses to decide whether a change is sound: checks that can be
disabled, counts with no basis, guarantees proved by substring. The high
ones target 0.2.15; the rest target 0.2.16.

**So the open work now reads:**

| target | entries | of which user-visible |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2.15 | 63 | 43 |
| 0.2.16 | 58 | 0 |
| 0.3.0 | 2 | — |
| 1.0.0 | 2 | — |
| unscheduled | 1 | — |

The 43 user-visible ones are the engine defects — what a user actually
meets — and they are what 0.2.15 is *for*. The other 20 are the
record-and-check work that shares the release.

**0.3.0's start condition is met**: every open user-visible defect names
a release. That was the condition, and it is now true.

### The scope, decided

**The line is whether a fix needs a design decision.**

A patch may spread across 0.2.15, 0.2.16 and beyond — that constraint
belongs to minors, not patches (`docs/PUBLISHING.md`). So the release
does not have to hold everything, and the question is only where to cut.

| | 0.2.15 | 0.2.16 |
|---|---|---|
| what | bounded fixes with a clear correct answer | fixes that need *which layer owns this* answered first |
| open entries | 46 | 69 |
| of which user-visible | 27 | the rest |

**Eleven moved to 0.2.16** — `024.99`, `024.129`, `024.28`, `024.44`,
`024.84`, `024.106`, `024.132`, `024.18`, `024.22`, `024.86`, `024.100`.
Each was assessed and found to need new machinery, a new source of
knowledge, or a decision about which layer answers.

`024.99` is the worked example. An implementation attempt classified it
small and produced a change touching **seven files under `core/lib`**.
That is not a bounded fix; it is a cross-cutting change to how visibility
reaches completion, and calling it small is how a fix gets made in seven
places instead of one.

**0.2.16 is where `024.151`'s two mechanisms belong**, and that is not a
coincidence: *every check states its own coverage*, and *wiring is proved
by execution rather than by text*. Most of what moved there is the same
shape one level down — an answer assembled at each reader instead of
produced once.

### What has landed so far

Read the register rather than this paragraph — `released-in: 0.2.15` is
the answer, and an earlier version of this section named `024.40` as
shipped while its entry still says `open`, which is the class of untruth
this release is named after. At the time of writing: `024.21`, `024.82`,
`024.111`, `024.127`, `024.128`, `024.131`, `024.133`, `024.134`,
`024.218`, `024.219`.

Each test-first; the ones that change what the engine asserts were
measured over real gem source, both sides on the same corpus digest at
different revisions.

**`024.111` earned a countermeasure, and this is where that is recorded.**
`#visit_block_node` was found in 0.2.13 and again in 0.2.15 — the same
place in two releases running, which `CLAUDE.md` says to answer with a
mechanism rather than a third hand fix. The mechanism is the
`CARRIED_OUT` and `CONTAINED` tables in
`core/spec/ovallsp/visibility_through_block_spec.rb`: the list of
constructs a self-sharing block carries out exists once, the examples are
generated from it, and `Cref#in_block`'s comment points at the table
instead of restating it. Restating it is precisely what let two copies of
a three-part claim disagree with the code for two releases (`024.219`).
Enumerating instead of patching the reported case immediately found two
more constructs — `protected`, and a `public` cancelling an open
`private` — that neither entry had ever mentioned.

**What remains in 0.2.15:**

1. Take the 57 in severity order and decide, per entry, whether it
   belongs to 0.2.15 or to a later patch. Re-target what does not fit.
   A patch may carry a handful of related fixes; it may not carry 57.
2. `024.151`'s 55 findings and round 3's untriaged remainder are **not
   entries yet**. Triaging them into entries comes first, because
   scoping a release against a list that is missing most of its content
   is the mistake `046`'s A0 was built to stop.
3. Only then is there a release to plan.

## What stays on 0.3.0

Two entries, and both are structural rather than defects:

- **`024.R7`** — index what the gems define. Five of the roadmap's nine
  0.3.0 promises depend on it, and it is the only one that needs the
  Runtime Agent to grow a responsibility.
- **`024.R9`** — the register outgrew its file. It stays *after* the
  accuracy line rather than before it: a register is cheapest to move
  when nothing is appending to it, and 0.2.15 will append heavily.

`045`'s ordering argument for the capability items survives intact; what
changed is that the defects blocking them are no longer inside the same
release as the capabilities.

## The condition 0.3.0 must meet to start

Every open user-visible defect names a release. That is now true, and
`core/spec/meta/deferred_findings_spec.rb` keeps it true — an open entry
without a `target:` fails, and `unscheduled` is a value that must be
argued in the entry's body rather than left as an absent key.
