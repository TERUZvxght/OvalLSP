# 047 — 0.2.15: the accuracy line, given a boundary

**0.2.15 exists because 0.3.0 could not be two things.** `045` calls
0.3.0 "the first release that may add capability" and lists nine
promises; the register simultaneously aimed 35 open defects at it. A
release cannot be the one that adds capability and the one that absorbs
everything unscheduled, and `024.153` measured what the absence of that
decision had produced.

## The rule this release runs under, and it is new

`docs/PUBLISHING.md` gained two clauses in 0.2.14, and both are the
maintainer's:

- **A patch closes a gap against *any* earlier claim**, not only the
  immediately preceding release's. Written as "the previous one" until
  then, which made a patch nearly impossible to construct: the only way
  to find a defect in `0.N.0` is to be working on `0.N+1`, by which
  point it belongs to a release two behind. Bug fixes had nowhere to go.
- **A minor ships with no open, user-visible defect that has no
  `target:`.** Anything not fixed either names the release that will fix
  it or becomes a `NOT YET` row.

The second is what makes the first coherent. The patch definition
presupposes an earlier release claimed more than it did, and nothing
constrained how much more — so the patch stream was structurally
guaranteed and each `0.N.0` was implicitly permitted to ship
known-broken.

## What this release currently holds, and why that is not yet a plan

**57 open entries target 0.2.15.** That is not a release. It is the
result of giving every previously untargeted entry a target, and of
moving the accuracy work off 0.3.0 — both correct individually, and
together they have relocated the queue rather than dissolved it.

**This is stated rather than smoothed over**, because the failure it
replaces was exactly a queue nobody could see: 26 entries in no release
at all, 18 of them user-visible and published as limitations with no one
undertaking to fix them. A visible queue with a target on every item is
better than an invisible one, and it is not the same thing as a scoped
release.

**So the first act of 0.2.15 is scoping**, and it is work, not
bookkeeping:

1. Take the 57 in severity order and decide, per entry, whether it
   belongs to 0.2.15 or to a later patch. Re-target what does not fit.
   A patch may carry a handful of related fixes; it may not carry 57.
2. `024.151`'s 55 findings and round 3's untriaged remainder are **not
   entries yet**. Triaging them into entries comes first, because
   scoping a release against a list that is missing most of its content
   is the mistake `046`'s A0 was built to stop.
3. Only then is there a release to plan.

## What stays on 0.3.0

Two entries, and both are structural rather than defects:

- **`024.R7`** — index what the gems define. Five of the roadmap's nine
  0.3.0 promises depend on it, and it is the only one that needs the
  Runtime Agent to grow a responsibility.
- **`024.R9`** — the register outgrew its file. It stays *after* the
  accuracy line rather than before it: a register is cheapest to move
  when nothing is appending to it, and 0.2.15 will append heavily.

`045`'s ordering argument for the capability items survives intact; what
changed is that the defects blocking them are no longer inside the same
release as the capabilities.

## The condition 0.3.0 must meet to start

Every open user-visible defect names a release. That is now true, and
`core/spec/meta/deferred_findings_spec.rb` keeps it true — an open entry
without a `target:` fails, and `unscheduled` is a value that must be
argued in the entry's body rather than left as an absent key.
